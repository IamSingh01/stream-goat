## What's new in 8.0.2

**Live TV works better with big public playlists** (iptv-org, free-codecs and similar):

- **Every channel shows.** Android's TV guide stopped at 400 channels and its group buttons at 40. They now go on as
  far as your lists do. (The Windows/Linux guide still asks you to narrow down with a group or a search past 250
  channels, and says so.)
- **Channels in several groups are under each one.** A playlist's `group-title="Animation;Kids"` used to count only as
  Animation, so in a **kids profile** some children's channels were missing. Now it's under both, and the cards show
  "Animation · Kids".
- **Check channels.** A new button on the Live TV page opens every channel's stream (12 at a time) and tells you which
  ones answer. It also follows HLS lists into their first quality, because a list often still answers when the video
  behind it is gone. Channels that don't answer are dimmed, with the reason ("gone (HTTP 404)", "no answer",
  "security certificate problem"…), and **Hide not working** leaves them out until the next check.
- **Backup addresses.** When a channel doesn't play, the player tries the same channel from your other playlists
  (matched by its tvg-id, where @SD and @HD count as one channel, or by name without quality marks). Its Sources
  menu lists them as "Backup from …".
- **Better playlist reading.**
  - Headers given as attributes on the channel line (`http-referrer`, `http-user-agent`, `http-origin`) are now used.
  - Lines a converter broke ("…like Gecko) Chrome/120…" left in the line) are repaired, so the channel gets the
    browser identity its server expects.
  - Channel names containing a comma ("Love Nature, 4K") are kept whole.
- **Android: choose a Live TV source** at the top of the Live TV page ("All sources ▾"), as on Windows and Linux:
  one playlist at a time, or all of them, plus Refresh and Live TV sources.
- **Android: add a playlist from a file.** Settings → Live TV → M3U playlist → **Choose a file** (.m3u, .m3u8). The
  file is copied into the app, so it keeps working if the original is moved.

**Report problems on GitHub.** After a crash, Android's report has a **Report on GitHub** button: GitHub's new-issue
page opens with the report filled in, and you press Submit (a GitHub account is needed). On a TV it shows a QR code to
open the page on your phone. Settings → About → **Report a problem** does the same without a crash, on Android,
Windows and Linux. Nothing is sent by the app itself.
